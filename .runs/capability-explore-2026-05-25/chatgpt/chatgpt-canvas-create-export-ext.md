# Probe Canvas

Machine learning has roots in the mid-20th century, when early computer scientists began asking whether machines could learn from data rather than simply follow fixed instructions. In the 1950s and 1960s, researchers developed foundational ideas such as neural networks, perceptrons, and symbolic artificial intelligence, setting the stage for decades of experimentation.

Progress came in waves. Interest rose when new algorithms showed promise, then cooled during periods when computers were too limited or expectations were too high. By the 1980s and 1990s, methods such as decision trees, support vector machines, and statistical learning helped machine learning become more practical for classification, prediction, and pattern recognition.

The modern era accelerated in the 2000s and 2010s as large datasets, powerful GPUs, and improved neural network techniques made deep learning highly effective. Breakthroughs in speech recognition, computer vision, translation, and later generative AI turned machine learning from a research specialty into a central technology shaping many fields today.
